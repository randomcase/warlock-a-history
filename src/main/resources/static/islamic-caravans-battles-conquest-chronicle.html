Canonical Islamic Caravans, Battles for Trade, Founding of Conquest & Recording of Conquest

القوافل القرآنية (رحلة الشتاء والصيف) • سرايا وغزوات التجارة • تأسيس الفتح المبين • تدوين المغازي وفتوح البلدان وديوان الخراج

In canonical Islamic history and jurisprudence, Conquest (Al-Fatḥ — "The Opening") did not emerge from arbitrary territorial ambition. It followed a strict, four-stage theological, economic, and historiographical progression rooted in the Qur'an and Prophetic Sīrah: from the Winter and Summer Caravans of Quraysh (Surah Quraysh 106:1–4), through the Battles for Trade (Nakhlah, Badr's Al-'Īr vs. An-Nafīr, and Al-Qaradah) that reclaimed confiscated Muhājirūn wealth and broke Meccan oligarchic blockades, to the Founding of Conquest (Ṣulḥ Al-Ḥudaybiyyah, Fatḥ Makkah, Tabūk's Aylah Charter, and the Ribāṭ-Caravanserai Commonwealth), culminating in the Canonical Recording of Conquest (Tadwīn Al-Maghāzī, 'Umar's Dīwān Al-Jund & Sawād Waqf Survey, Al-Wāqidī, Al-Balādhurī's Futūḥ Al-Buldān, and Ottoman Tapu Tahrir Defters).

1. Canonical Caravans (Al-'Īr & Īlāf)

Hāshim's Īlāf safe-conduct treaties (Qur'an 106:1–4) linked Yemen's winter frankincense/spice route to Syria's summer grain/olive-oil route—until Quraysh forfeited their covenant by expelling the believers and weaponizing caravan capital.

2. Battles for Trade (Al-'Īr vs. An-Nafīr)

Medina projected sovereignty across the Red Sea coast (Sarāyā & Nakhlah, Qur'an 2:217), met Abu Sufyan's 1,000-camel caravan and Abu Jahl's army at Badr (Qur'an 8:7), and sealed the Iraqi silver route at Al-Qaradah.

3. Founding of Conquest (Ta'sīs Al-Fatḥ)

Al-Ḥudaybiyyah (Qur'an 48:1) and Fatḥ Makkah replaced pagan toll-extortion and Ribā with bloodless amnesty (At-Ṭulaqā'), while Tabūk's Aylah treaty guaranteed safe-conduct for ships at sea and caravans on land.

4. Recording of Conquest (At-Tadwīn & Dīwān)

Prophetic silver-sealed leather charters, Caliph 'Umar's 36M-Jarīb Sawād Waqf cadastre (Qur'an 59:7–10), Al-Wāqidī's field topography, and Al-Balādhurī's Ṣulḥan vs. 'Anwatan constitutional ledger.
