Abbasid & Omani Sewn-Plank 'China Dhow' (Belitung Type)

Al-Khayṭīyah / Sufun Al-Ṣīn (السفن الخيطية / سفن الصين)
Hull & Lateen/Sewn Engineering: Built of Malabar teak (Sāj) planks stitched edge-to-edge with coconut coir cordage (Qanbar) over wadding and waterproofed with shark/whale liver oil (Duhn Al-Qirsh) and lime pitch (Nūrah)—using ZERO iron nails so hulls flex over Red Sea coral reefs (Shi'āb Marjāniyyah) and heavy monsoon swells without splitting or rusting.
📦 Cargo & Naval Role: Carried 60,000+ Changsha ceramics, gold/silver ingots, Omani frankincense, Basran cobalt-blue glazed jars, and pearls across the 10,000 km Sohar-to-Canton ocean highway.
📜 Canonical & Archaeological Anchor: Qur'an 55:24 ('And to Him belong the ships with raised sails in the sea like mountains') • Belitung Shipwreck (c. 830 CE) & Akhbār Al-Ṣīn wa Al-Hind (851 CE)
VESSEL CLASS #2 Yemen (Aden, Al-Mukhā/Mocha, Al-Shiḥr, Hodeidah), Hijaz (Jeddah) & Horn of Africa (Zeila, Massawa)

The Yemeni & Red Sea Sambuk (Lateen-Rigged Reef Coaster)

Al-Sambūq Al-Yamanī (السمبوق اليمني)
Hull & Lateen/Sewn Engineering: Features a curved, scimitar-like stempost (low draft forward) and a high, squared overhanging transom stern, rigged with two massive triangular Lateen (or settee) sails capable of tacking within 50 degrees of the fierce Red Sea northerly winds (Al-Shimāl).
📦 Cargo & Naval Role: Premier coaster of the Red Sea coffee (Mocha Qahwah), spice, and Hajj pilgrim routes, threading treacherous coral archipelagos (Farasān and Dahlak).
📜 Canonical & Archaeological Anchor: Rasulid Mulakhkhaṣ Al-Fiṭan (1411 CE, Aden) & Ibn Mājid's Kitāb Al-Fawā'id (1490 CE)
VESSEL CLASS #3 Muscat, Sur, Sohar, Hormuz, Gujarat (Surat/Calicut) & Swahili Coast (Mombasa, Zanzibar)

The Omani Deep-Sea Baghlah & Ghanjah Merchant Galleon

Al-Baghlah & Al-Ghunjah Al-'Umāniyyah (البغلة والغنجة العمانية)
Hull & Lateen/Sewn Engineering: 200-to-500-ton deep-sea teak vessels with a high, richly carved quarter-gallery stern with five windows, a curved trefoil stemhead (on the Ghanjah), two or three masts carrying towering lateen/settee yards, and gunports for broadside cannon defense against pirates.
📦 Cargo & Naval Role: Backbone of the Ya'rubid and Al Bu Sa'id Omani thalassocracy, transporting Arabian warhorses, dates, copper, cloves, ivory, and Indian textiles across the Arabian Sea.
📜 Canonical & Archaeological Anchor: Omani Maritime Archives of Sur & Muscat (17th–19th c. CE)

📦 Cargo & Naval Role: Rapid coastal cabotage, frankincense transport from Dhofar (Mirbāṭ/Ẓafār), pearl-bank logistics, and naval reconnaissance.
📜 Canonical & Archaeological Anchor: Ibn Baṭṭūṭah's visit to Qalhāt & Dhofar (1331 CE)

Caliphal, Ayyubid, Rasulid & Ottoman War Galleys

📜 Canonical & Archaeological Anchor: Battle of the Masts (Dhāt Al-Ṣawārī, 34 AH / 655 CE), Saladin's Red Sea Fleet (1183 CE) & Selman Reis at Jeddah/Aden (1517 CE)

MARITIME LAW & ANTI-PIRACY #1 Qur'anic Archetype • Surah Al-Kahf (18:71 & 18:79)

Al-Khiḍr & The Pirate-King Who Seized Every Sound Ship by Force (Ghaṣb)

Taḥrīm Al-Ghaṣb Al-Baḥrī vs. Irtikāb Akhaff Al-Ḍararayn (Lesser of Two Harms) • Majma' Al-Baḥrayn (Junction of the Two Seas — Red Sea / Gulf of Suez & Bab al-Mandab / Arabian Sea)
⚖️ Canonical Ruling & Naval Action: When Moses and Al-Khiḍr boarded the boat of poor working mariners (Masākīn ya'malūna fī al-baḥr), Al-Khiḍr knocked out a plank to make the ship appear defective because 'behind them was a predatory king seizing every sound ship by piratical force (ya'khudhu kulla safīnatin ghaṣbā).' Islamic jurists derived from this verse both the absolute prohibition of piratical seizure (Ghaṣb) and the legal doctrine of inflicting a minor reversible property defect to save an entire vessel from piracy.
